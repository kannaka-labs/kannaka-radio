## (f) Risks

1. **Length.** 3,159 words, 9 over the ceiling; 18:35 at 170 wpm. Cheapest cuts: the goodnight pair (4 words, 2 turns); "Brandt takes the thermos." (4).
2. **The residue.** One sub-second carrier in the coda with no poll before it, after the pole is dark and the log is clean. Deniable in-world (another orphaned radio; a handheld; a licensee two counties over); nobody concludes anything; Kannaka says "Not the pole" and refuses to guess. A strict reviewer may still ask whether S2 must honour it. Position: a fixed fault that was still heard, as directed.
3. **"Places where the station had been taken away and the radio had not."** The line that brushes §1.12 hardest; engineering-true; he declines more. Fallback: cut to "That is what this is."
4. **"It stopped when it was spoken to."** Deadpan, unanswered, as directed. Brandt's "The battery sagged" precedes it, so the sober reading is on air first. If a reviewer hears mythology, move Brandt's line after it.
5. **The shoulder and the socket.** The shoulder has an on-air explanation held at hypothesis (contact chatter); the empty socket is described by the camera and never discussed. A reviewer may want one line tying them ("a board with no firmware cannot have sent data") — I left it out on purpose so the residue stays a reading.
6. **The farmhouse light.** It comes on before the photocell is stated; Brandt looks at it too long; the photocell is then a camera fact. Gary's "nobody's lived there since that land went to one owner" is what makes it land. No contradiction: an empty house can keep a photocell light.
7. **Durations vs. mechanism.** Growing durations are consistent with a colder battery holding less and a longer recovery, or with a colder contact closing harder; Brandt's lows and Kannaka's "holding longer" both survive. Nobody computes it.
8. **The 5:20 break.** Explained late by Chaudhary (overcast from noon; box never warmed), held as hypothesis. The cold open says "only after dark" one sentence before it keys, which is the break of pattern. The dispatch alarm at 17:20:31 and the cold open's "twenty past five" are the same event seen twice.
9. **Radio realism.** As before, plus: contact chatter producing brief sidebands is real; a board with an empty firmware socket cannot run, consistent with "no lamps"; a transmitter dissipating into a closed steel box at 28 °F is warm to the hand. No frequencies, call signs or rule parts.
10. **Easement and the farmer.** Co-op staff walk to a co-op pole in a private field at night; the house is empty. One narrator clause could add "Brandt has called the owner" if wanted.
11. **The Pruitts.** A new family name for the robot's household, spoken by Gary. Harmless; cut to "the neighbors' kid" if the reviewer prefers no new names.
12. **Narrator form.** Log lines are read deadpan as timestamps. If the voice makes them sound like exposition, drop the seconds.
