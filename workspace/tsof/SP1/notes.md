# TSOF SP1 — "The Trials of Kannaka" — writer's notes

Standalone special, outside season numbering. Script: `workspace/tsof/SP1/script.txt` (plain text, no SFX lines). Not rendered, not posted, no canon doc edited.

## (a) When it is set, and why that fits aired E08–E12

**A weekday in mid-July 2026** (the hearing). The trespass ran Monday to Wednesday nights of the week before.

- **The year.** S1 is 2026. E05 L95: Ada dates the unsent fragment "March, oh-seven", and E03 L149 has Ada say it was typed "nineteen years ago". E12 L344 gives the Halvorsen year 2003 as "the year of her birth", and Teodora is 23 in S2 (E09 L5), which puts S2 in January 2027.
- **The gap.** E08's operation was in March. E10 L73, Ada: "When did you see him last?" / "March." S2 opens "Ten months since…" (E09 L68), on 15 January (E09 L2). July therefore sits inside the gap.
- **Kannaka was in Cedar Rapids.** Nothing aired puts him anywhere else in the gap.
  - In January he lives "in a rented room above a laundromat on First Avenue East" (E09 L98).
  - He says he has wanted to tell Ada "every night at eleven minutes past ten" (E12 L326). That implies ten months near bay six; it does not require him to be there every night.
- **He wrote nothing in the gap.** E09 L101 aired: "since then the file has held forty-one entries and one amendment and has not grown." So the special's ledger is **spoken and never written**. It opens "Note to file. ...No, not to file." and closes "It is not in the file. Nothing tonight is." This departs from the brief, which asked for the file he is finishing. Aired canon wins: any written entry would break E09. The number "forty-one" is deliberately not spoken (see E08/E09 entry-count fallibility).
- **Gary is on third shift.** He moved to days only "in the fall" (E10 L95). In July he is still on thirds, which is why he turns up for daytime jury duty in coveralls with his lunch pail, straight off a shift.
- **Brandt is still in post** (she retires late January, E11 L89). No year count is spoken: she said 31 in S1 and 32 in E11.
- **No 22:11 anywhere**, so the special does not touch the S2 appointment motif.
- **Nothing contradicts the season or heat.** Every aired scene from S1 E01 to S2 E12 is February, March or January. The only aired summers are Teodora's backstory (E12).

**S1 Five Questions.**
- **Q1 (Flaukowski human before legend):** YES. He is absent, and nothing in SP1 mythologises him; Chaudhary's "a project earlier this year. It has concluded" is the only oblique reference.
- **Q2 (Kannaka antagonistic before understandable):** YES. He is shown committing a real safety violation, the co-op's evidence is accepted, and his motive stays unknowable all the way through the coda.
- **Q3 (an intelligent viewer could disagree):** YES. Brandt has a sound safety case ("linemen with twenty years in who won't stand where he stood without insulated gloves on"). He has a reason he will pay for and not spend. The magistrate rules for both: guilty and fined, but free to stand on the public road.
- **Q4 (the impossible through engineering and records):** YES. Every anomaly arrives through a record:
  - the camera recording against the foreman's written statement;
  - a 1959-stamped photo badge of the same man;
  - the system's default DOB of 01/01/1900.

  Nobody remarks on any of them as uncanny.
- **Q5 (begins the myth without explaining it away):** YES. The special adds procedure, not mythology. The "right" substation is never named, and the why stays sealed.

## (f) Risks I am not sure about

1. **Brandt's E11 behaviour.** In January (E11 L272) Brandt says "he signs in, under whatever name he's got", then "reads the signature, reads it again, and says nothing" (L287). After SP1 she has sat through his hearing and knows the name. I read E11 as consistent: the citation's name existed in two spellings, W and no W, which fits both "whatever name he's got" and the second reading. A strict reviewer may call it a seam. The fallback is to cut the "Have we met?" exchange and have Brandt send the lineman's report instead of attending, but that loses the strongest scene.
2. **Teodora's audit.** In E10–E11 she pulls every county record on him (L164 plat, photos) and never mentions a July trespass case, which would be a public court record in the same county. Not mentioning it is not a contradiction, but it is a gap a listener could notice. E13+ could let her find it, or just never mention it.
3. **Humour budget.** The premise is comic-procedural, so the script runs about 8 deadpan beats against the normal budget of one:
   - the W;
   - "That one is wrong" / "a number of dates it is not";
   - "It is a good likeness";
   - the hat as the motion alarm;
   - "The fence was a fence";
   - "the fence, or the road";
   - Chaudhary's "control";
   - the change "four dollars short".

   None is a staccato tag, and none is at anyone's expense; the foreman and the lineman are both defended. Nick may want to trim to four or five.
4. **Legal realism.**
   - Iowa magistrates can take a simple-misdemeanor guilty plea with this colloquy.
   - A court-issued no-trespass order covering all of the co-op's facilities may not be standard Iowa relief. More often the owner serves a notice and the court notes it.
   - The surcharge wording is generic.
   - Iowa's critical-infrastructure statutes could classify utility trespass differently. I gave no dollar amounts or statute numbers on purpose.
5. **The crew walking past him.** The camera shows him standing by the control house while the foreman passes within ten feet twice. The foreman's statement says nobody else was in the yard. The scene is grounded and fits S1's unseen watcher (E01) and E10's watched-but-unapproached pattern, but a reviewer could call it a new capability. If so, soften it to the foreman "was facing the regulator".
6. **Chaudhary discloses "a project earlier this year" in open court,** with Brandt looking at him, against the inquiry's "no personnel on site". It names nothing, but it is the one line that brushes S1's closed record.
7. **Gary's jury list saying he "already served… in twenty-nineteen"** deliberately evokes the memorial-card year. It is residue, not a death record, but it reopens 2019 on air.
8. **"The old seven" and standing outside it on the public road** implies an ongoing watch at a specific, unnamed substation. Listeners may assume it is the south yard; the script never says so, and nothing confirms it.
9. **His car.** He has one in S2 (E09, E11) but shows no driver's license here. A listener may wonder; the script never raises it.
10. **The badge as a new prop.** A 1959-stamped photo ID of the same man is a new artefact. It is consistent with the aired 1958 photo (E05/E11), but it is the most "impossible" object in the special. The magistrate's "Your father's?" / "your grandfather's badge" keeps it ordinary.
11. **Departure from the brief:** the ledger is spoken only and not entered in the file, for the reason in (a).
