## BLOCKING

### B1. The foreman's statement makes a co-op report that does not reconcile, in July. This contradicts aired canon.
Script L122: "My foreman's written statement says nobody was in the yard but the crew." Against the camera recording, that is a co-op incident file in which a human statement disagrees with an instrument, and it sits on Brandt's desk in July. Aired lines:
- E08 L161 (narrator): "Brandt's reports reconcile. All of them, perfectly, forever after — and she reads every one … the way you read a witness who has been coached."
- E09 L68 (narrator): "the county has spent them the way a county does: the inquiry closed its file, the co-op's reports reconciled, the snow came back."
- E11 L71/L77 (Brandt): "Every one of them agrees with every other one, to the minute … This one doesn't reconcile with anything. First one in ten months … It's the first honest reading I've had since March."

The line also gives Kannaka a new capability: unseen in daylight, at ten feet, by a working crew. The writer flagged this as risk 5.

Nights 2 and 3 have a related gap (L131). The script establishes that the gate was locked at four and then finds him inside, without saying how he got in. A listener can hear that he passed a locked fence, which breaks his aired condition. E07 L55: "doors. From tonight you use them, all of them, in both directions, like a person." E11 L173: "The habit has outlived the asking."

**Replace L122 with:**
> He didn't get in after four. I pulled the recording. He walks in through the open gate at one-forty in the afternoon, past my crew, and stands by the control house, and he stands there fourteen hours. Twice my foreman walks within ten feet of him. My foreman's written statement says he saw an older gentleman in a coat by the control house and took him for somebody from engineering, because nobody gets inside that fence who isn't supposed to be there. Those are his words, and he isn't proud of them.

**In L131, after "Tuesday night", add the entry route:**
> Tuesday he came in at the gate again with my crew in the afternoon, the recording has it, and that night my lineman went back at four on purpose and found him in the same place …

Wednesday needs the same treatment: "Wednesday he came in with the crew again. My lineman went out at midnight and sat in his truck outside the fence …"

Keep L128 unchanged ("The recording is accurate. The foreman's statement is also accurate, as to what he saw. He was looking at his work…"). It still lands, and lands better, because the foreman's mistake is now ordinary.

### B2. "Seven" is the number of the thing he is watching for. That pairs two S2 §8 items.
L164, L170, L173 and L305 ("substation seven", "which substation was seven?", "The old seven"). The episode's spine is Kannaka's refused reason for watching, which is §1.12 and S2 §8 ("Kannaka's full motivation: the ledger, **the seven**, the complete why"). The object of that watch is a site called "seven". Internally, seven is the count of his ledger's successes (§1.12: "has worked seven times"). On air, sevens are already loaded: E12's "seven" from the breakwall, "Seven of the forty-two", S1 §4's seven possibilities, and Teodora's seven letters. The script names nothing and closes nothing. But a special whose whole content is "why does he watch, and what" should not hand listeners the ledger's number as the answer to "what". The fix costs one word.

**Replace** every "seven" in L164/L170/L173/L305 with **"twenty-eight"** (it never appears in E01–E12):
> L164: Ms. Brandt, you called Otis Road substation twenty-eight. Since when has it had that number?
> L170: And before nineteen eighty-eight, which substation was twenty-eight?
> L173: Before that, twenty-eight was — why?

Avoid any of these numbers in the revision: 7, 11, 14, 18, 19, 22, 33, 40, 41, 42, 76, 206.

### B3. A lineman cannot write a State of Iowa citation, and a co-op does not send one alone to an intruder at 4 a.m.
L5 ("The lineman who wrote it…"), L98, L116 and L131. In Iowa a criminal citation is issued by a peace officer (Iowa Code ch. 805). A utility employee calls the sheriff. A co-op security procedure, especially since the 2022 substation attacks, never sends a lone on-call lineman into a fence to confront an unknown person on the third night running. Anyone in the trade would wince at this. It also undercuts Q4 ("believable engineering").

**Replacements:**
> L5: … The deputy who wrote it did so at four in the morning with a flashlight held in his teeth, and his handwriting is better than mine. He has spelled my name with a W. …
> L98: Ms. Brandt, is this the man in your complaint?
> L116: At three fifty-two Tuesday morning the intrusion camera flagged motion inside the fence. My on-call lineman drove out with a sheriff's deputy, and they found this man standing beside the control house with his hat in his hands. The motion the camera flagged was him taking the hat off. The deputy asked him to leave. The report says, and I'm reading: "He said of course, and asked whether he might use the gate."
> L131: … found him in the same place, and the deputy gave him a written trespass warning, and he read it twice and handed it back. Wednesday … At four in the morning the lineman unlocked it and walked in with the deputy, and the deputy wrote the citation. His report says the man told him, "Good morning. I expected you."
> L17: Present, Your Honor. The name is Nowakoski, without the W. (also cut per B4)
> L227: Mr. Nowakoski, if you tell me why, I'll ask for this to be dismissed today. I mean that. …

If "his handwriting is better than mine" goes under B4, cut it here too.

## 3. FIVE QUESTIONS (S1 §9, adapted for a standalone special)

1. **Flaukowski human before legend? YES.** He is absent. The special adds no mythology about him, and the only reference is a concluded "project".
2. **Kannaka antagonistic before understandable? YES.** He commits a genuine safety violation three nights running, refuses a decent official her one request, and will not say whether her stations are at risk. His motive stays opaque through the coda.
3. **Would an intelligent viewer disagree about who is right? YES.** Brandt's safety case ("I've been to funerals in this trade") and her "cause" box against his paid-for, unspent reason. The magistrate splits it (guilty, but the road stays public). N1 keeps Brandt's case technically accurate.
4. **Impossible through believable engineering? YES, conditional on B1 and B3.** Every anomaly enters through a record: the camera, the 1959 badge, the 01/01/1900 default, the renumbering. B3 removes the procedural error that would undercut "believable".
5. **Begins the myth without explaining it away? YES.** The right substation is unnamed, the error is unexplained ("a note that guesses is worse than no note"), and the why stays sealed.

## 5. REALISM

- **Blocking:** B3 (lineman citation and lone confrontation).
- **N1 (recommended): Brandt's safety line overstates the hazard.** L134: "linemen with twenty years in who won't stand where he stood without insulated gloves on and a hot stick between them and the bus". He stood by the control house, which is usually the least hazardous spot in the yard. Gloves and hot sticks are for working energized parts, not for standing. Co-op listeners will hear Brandt exaggerate, and that weakens her for Q3. Replace with: "I'm here because inside that fence the air will carry current if you give it a reason to. My linemen with twenty years in don't walk that yard without a tailboard briefing, a hard hat, and somebody who knows where they are, and he stood in it fourteen hours with nobody who knew who he was. I've been to funerals in this trade, and I'd like to go to no more of them than I have to."
- **N2 (recommended): the no-trespass "order" on a fine-only plea.** An Iowa magistrate imposing a fine has no probation hook for a stay-away order. The realistic mechanism is the owner's written no-trespass notice (Iowa Code §716.7: entering after notice is trespass), noted on the record. Edits:
  - L275: "…the clerk downstairs will give you the total. The cooperative has served you a written no-trespass notice covering all of its fenced facilities in the county, for one year, and asks that I note it on the record. Do you understand it?"
  - L278: "…Does the notice cover the fence, or the road?"
  - L281: "Inside the fence is covered. The public road is the public road."
  - L290: "Ms. Brandt, nobody can keep a man off a public road for standing on it."
  - L296: "So noted, and we're done, Mr. Nowakoski. Stay outside the fences."
  - L317: "…for her copy of the record."
  - L365: "There is a notice, and the court has noted it. … I have read it three times, and that is what it says."

  Every beat survives.
- **N3: hearing structure.** The complainant and a consultant present evidence and the defendant is invited to "ask the complainant questions" (L161), yet L245 says the cooperative will "bring the lineman and the recording" only at trial. That is internally inconsistent: at an arraignment nobody testifies. Lightest fix:
  - after L110, the magistrate: "Before you plead, Mr. Nowakoski, I'll hear what this is about."
  - L161: "Mr. Nowakoski, before you plead, is there anything you want to ask about what's been said?"
  - L245: "…the State will have to prove it at trial, with the lineman, the deputy and the recording…"
- **N4: Mays Island geography.** The Linn County Courthouse is on Mays Island, which is crossed by three bridges (1st, 2nd and 3rd Avenue), not "a bridge at each end" (L8). The Linn County Recorder is, to my knowledge, in the Jean Oxley Public Service Center on 2nd Street SW, west of the river, so L359 "walks east across the bridge toward the recorder's office" points the wrong way. Make it "west", or drop the direction. Check before render.
- **N5: statute class.** Several states raised trespass on critical infrastructure or utility property above a simple misdemeanor after 2017–2022. If Iowa's §716.8 does this for a fenced substation, the case would not be in magistrate's court. No statute is spoken, but L5 says "simple misdemeanor" outright. Either verify it, or drop the two words from L5 and leave L11's statistic generic.
- The 01/01/1900 default is plausible for a legacy case-management system. The plea colloquy is fine for a fine-only simple misdemeanor (no counsel advisory is needed without jail exposure, which is another reason not to fix N2 with a suspended jail term).
